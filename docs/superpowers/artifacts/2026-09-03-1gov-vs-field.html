Security posture, honestly compared

Only three products in this comparison document any defense against prompt injection in mail — and the two big ones have both been publicly bypassed. The honest claim is not "immune"; it is "smaller blast radius, verifiable claims."

Microsoft Copilot

  • XPIA classifier blocks malicious instructions in grounding data; Defender ships injection detections.
  • Bypassed in the wild: CVE-2025-32711 EchoLeak zero-click exfiltration, then CVE-2026-26133 summary hijacking. Both patched.
  • Deep agentic reach (Graph, files, calendar) means a successful injection touches far more than mail.

Google Gemini

  • Published six-layer defense: classifiers, security thought reinforcement, sanitization, confirmations, notifications, adversarial training.
  • Documented incident: hidden white-on-white text injected a fake "security alert" into an email summary.
  • Google's own framing: continuous mitigation, not a solved problem.

1Gov Mail

  • Structural, not semantic: per-message sentinel fencing, injection-attempt detection with a visible ⚠ badge, and a briefing composer that never sees raw email text — only length-clamped extracted fields.
  • Every briefing claim links to its source message; AI output only ever lands in a human-reviewed draft. Nothing is actioned autonomously.
  • Measured limits are documented in the code — including where small-model defenses fail — instead of marketing assurances. Blast radius is inherently bounded: one tenant, no agentic actions, model behind the firewall.

Where the field is ahead

An honest benchmark cuts both ways. Four capabilities are shipped and mature in several competitors and absent or partial in 1Gov Mail today — three of them are already sequenced on the roadmap.

Chat with your inbox ROADMAP · PHASE 4

Copilot, Gemini, Superhuman, Shortwave, Zia, and Spark all answer natural-language questions over mail. Our card pipeline is the designed foundation; retrieval infrastructure is the missing piece.

Persistent AI triage labels ROADMAP · PHASE 2

Superhuman Auto Labels, Copilot Prioritize-my-inbox, Canary's on-device triage run continuously. Our briefing computes the same classifications per-run; phase 2 persists them as inbox labels and filters.

Commitment / follow-up tracking ROADMAP · PHASE 3

Superhuman's Follow Up Faster is the reference. The briefing's "you promised / waiting on you" sections become a persistent ledger in phase 3.

Semantic search ROADMAP · PHASE 4

Most of the field ships natural-language search. Ours is keyword-based; a self-hosted embedding model would be required to close this without leaving the network.

Frontier-model output quality STRUCTURAL TRADE-OFF

Competitors ride GPT/Gemini/Claude-class models. Our self-hosted qwen3-30b is capable but not frontier-class; quality scales with whatever hardware and open-weight models the government chooses to deploy — a tunable, not a ceiling.

The trade is explicit: the field buys quality and breadth by sending mail to someone else's model. 1Gov Mail buys sovereignty, Kinyarwanda, and auditability by running its own — and closes the breadth gap on a roadmap it controls.
