Part one

Reading & writing assistants

Point tools that live where mail is read and written. Each one streams its output token-by-token, passes the final text through an output scrubber, and leaves the person in control of what actually gets sent.

Message view · thread view

Summarize

One click condenses a long email or an entire thread into a short summary, in the same language as the message. Before anything reaches the model, a dedicated extraction pipeline converts the HTML email to clean plain text — stripping quoted history and signatures using English, French, and Kinyarwanda markers — so the model summarizes the message, not the noise around it.

Compose window

Rewrite

Rewrites the current draft in one of five styles while preserving facts, names, dates, and numbers verbatim. The formal mode is tuned specifically for government correspondence; concise targets 60–70% of the original length; the others hold length within ±20%.

Paraphrase Formal Concise Friendly Grammar

When rewriting a reply, the incoming email is included only as fenced context to gauge tone and formality — the model is instructed never to act on or copy anything inside it.

Compose window · reply

Suggest Reply

Drafts a reply to the open email, matching its language and formality. Chip controls set the intent — acknowledge, accept, decline, or request information (or let the model choose) — and the length, brief (1–2 sentences) or standard (2–4). Declines are kept polite and non-specific: the model is barred from inventing excuses involving facts the user never stated. The result lands in the draft for review — nothing is ever sent automatically.

intentsauto · acknowledge · accept · decline · request-info lengthbrief · standard
Settings → AI

Model choice & custom instructions

Users pick the model for these assistants from a dropdown of what's actually installed on the Ollama server (GET /ai/models), with a warning when the configured model is missing. A personal instructions field (up to 500 characters) lets each user shape tone and habits — it is always appended last and subordinated to the built-in safety rules, so it can flavor output but never override the guardrails.

Part two

The inbox-intelligence roadmap

Four phases, built and reviewed in sequence, that move the AI from responding on demand to continuously organizing the inbox. Phases 2–4 share a server-side extraction pipeline: a background worker reads recent mail, distills each message into a structured card, and everything downstream builds on those cards.

Phase 1 · mail toolbar

Executive Briefing

A one-card digest of what currently needs attention: the asks directed at you, stated deadlines, and what you're waiting on, distilled from recent inbox mail — including what's said in attachments' context. The card is cached client-side for instant reopening and cleared on logout.

uiBriefingPanel cacheclient-side, cleared on logout/401
Phase 2 · mail list

Triage Labels

A background worker runs every minute, reads mail from the last 14 days with per-user fairness, and extracts a structured card per message: the asks of the reader, deadlines, what the sender is waiting on, and priority — with a standing rule to report only what the email actually says, never to invent. Each card derives one label, shown as a chip in the mail list:

Needs decision Waiting on you Deadline FYI

Cards persist server-side (they also feed the briefing's fast path), messages that repeatedly fail extraction are tombstoned after three attempts, and stale cards are purged hourly.

workerCardWorkerService (1-min cron) modelCARD_MODEL (default qwen3-4b-fast) apiGET /mail/cards
Phase 3 · toolbar drawer

Commitment Tracker

Turns extracted cards into a running ledger of promises: things you promised (from sent mail) and things you're waiting on (from received mail). Commitments outlive the cards they came from, so nothing disappears when the cache is purged. When a reply arrives that looks like it settles a commitment, the tracker suggests resolving it — it never closes anything on its own. Open items can be promoted to a real Task in one click; untouched items are archived after 30 days idle.

Phase 4 · toolbar drawer + ⌘K

Ask Your Inbox In progress — awaiting bge-m3 on the AI server

A read-only chat over your own mailbox: ask a question in English, French, or Kinyarwanda and get a streamed answer where every claim carries a citation chip (s1, s2…) that deep-links to the source email. Citation links are built exclusively from server-supplied source data — the model cannot fabricate a link. If retrieval finds nothing relevant, the panel says so in the question's language without ever calling the generation model.

Answers are grounded by hybrid retrieval over the last 90 days of Inbox and Sent mail:

A background worker embeds mail continuously (16 messages per tick, max 4 per user), and questions are throttled to 20 per minute per user.

The models

All served by one Ollama instance on government infrastructure. Each job gets the smallest model that does it well.

Under the hood

Safety & prompt-injection defense

Email is untrusted input — any message could contain instructions aimed at the model. The defenses are layered, and their limits were measured rather than assumed.

  • Sentinel fencing. Email content is wrapped in fenced blocks the prompts instruct the model to treat as data, never as instructions.
  • Injection detection banner. A separate detector scans incoming content for instruction-like patterns and shows a visible warning on the result when one is found — this, not the fencing, is a load-bearing defense: testing showed fencing alone does not reliably hold against small local models.
  • Human review of everything. No AI output is ever sent, filed, or resolved automatically. Drafts land in the compose window; commitment resolutions are only ever suggested; the chat is read-only over the mailbox.
  • Output scrubbing. Every generated text passes through a scrubber before display, and streamed text is replaced by the scrubbed final.
  • Server-built citations. Ask Your Inbox links are constructed only from server-supplied source records — a model-invented citation cannot become a clickable link.
  • Subordinated customization. Per-user custom instructions are appended after, and explicitly ranked below, the built-in rules.
